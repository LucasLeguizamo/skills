# Session 11 — Building Your Dream Team (Sesión 11 — Construyendo tu Dream Team)

The problem: you can't keep up the content volume alone, or you want to scale. The answer: hire to remove today's specific bottleneck, not to fill an ideal org chart. Keep the squad small (about 5), make AI part of every role, and recruit for agency and proof of past work.

## Key ideas

- **"Hire what you need, not what you want"** (a well-known creator's line, quoted in class). Hire to solve a problem, not to build an ideal team. Every day, ask what is stopping growth right now and whether one hire would unblock it.
- **Typical bottlenecks, in rough order:**
  1. *Routine*: content isn't part of your life yet (e.g. only ~3 videos/week, no time to record). Fix this first, maybe with someone who comes to you to record.
  2. *Tasks you hate*: editing, metrics and so on. Hand off what you dislike and keep doing what you enjoy.
  3. *Connection*: you aren't reaching the audience. This is usually a format and avatar problem (weak formats, poorly copied, too little volume), not a production problem.
  4. *Quality*: "better" means results against your original goal (leads, clients), not just views or followers. The missing skill may be understanding your customer, not better production.
  5. *Volume*: the biggest lever. Ask "how do I post double?" and hire whatever gets you there (editor, assistant, videographer, designer) if you can afford it.
- **Locate the bottleneck in the pipeline.** Recording: not enough raw footage for the editor. Editing: lots of footage, no time or skill to edit it. Analysis: formats aren't reviewed fast enough, so you can't tell what's working. Coordination: nobody keeps recording, editing, publishing and analysis moving, so you need a right hand or manager.
- **Think in skills, not people.** A social team covers six skills. They exist before the people do, and one person can cover several early on:
  1. Strategist: why you make content, what the account should achieve, where it's going. Usually the creator.
  2. Recording.
  3. Editing: turning footage into a video that works on social.
  4. Distribution / content manager: publishing on the right platforms at the right time, reliably ("como un relojito", like clockwork).
  5. Metrics: per-video results, which audience it reached, whether it brought the right followers and leads, and where to optimize.
  6. Design: carousels, thumbnails, static images.
- **AI cuts across every role.** Each role should be asking how to automate its own work. The instructor would not hire anyone today who is weak at AI. Mature teams add a dedicated person whose only job is turning what humans do into automated workflows.
- **One person can do it all.** The instructor grew a personal account from 0 to ~1.7M followers doing every role himself, at about one video a day, with time to spare and before AI tools. If you think it's impossible, you likely haven't systematized it yet.
- **A content-only hire can be underloaded.** Someone who only posts one video a day may have the easiest job in the company, so combine content with other marketing or performance work. Small companies often have one marketing person who also records, edits and posts content without trouble.
- **Hire for a culture of testing and shipping.** Someone close to execution beats a senior, directive profile who has never made content.
- **Suggested first hire: a videographer-editor in one person.** You want someone fast, flexible and agile who makes good short-form video, not a cinema-grade filmmaker. Speed and adapting to you matter more than polish. Split the role into two hires later, when volume saturates it.
- **Alternative first hire for solo founders:** a young, sharp "right hand" who takes the most draining work off you.
- **Editing is the most automatable role today.** With a good AI pipeline, one editor can do what used to need two.
- **The hardest role to fill is the one that "duplicates" the strategist:** someone who knows the creator well enough to pick formats and draft scripts on their own, so the creator only polishes and records.
- **Speed needs one decision-maker.** Ideally the owner is the person accountable for content and distributes the work.
- **Ideal squad when scaling (~5 people):** creator/strategist, content manager (the strategist's right hand), videographer (records at your place on your schedule and can help draft scripts), editor (footage comes back finished), designer. Add an AI specialist if needed, but ideally everyone knows AI.
- **Small teams beat big teams.** More people does not mean more speed, quality or output, because big teams struggle to make decisions. In a political campaign, a team of about 6 out-produced and out-performed a 70-person communications department.
- **The bottleneck loop:** find the bottleneck → solve it yourself or hire for it → document what the role does (so it can later go to AI or the next hire) → measure the impact (more videos, followers, leads) → find the next bottleneck.
- **Production matters more than planning.** At one video a day, about 10 minutes is enough to plan the week. The value is in the review: what worked, what didn't, and what to do next week.
- **Experimentation rule:** keep a proven format that is easy to repeat, and test something new in about 1 of every 10 videos.
- **If carousels outperform video on your account and serve your goal, make more carousels.** Don't limit yourself to video.
- **Andrés Bilbao's 10 practices of a high-performance culture:**
  1. Review limiting beliefs.
  2. Talent density (his own team was short on copy talent).
  3. Psychological safety.
  4. Truth and radical candor, with constant feedback.
  5. Striking, high-frequency rules.
  6. Vulnerability as a source of cohesion.
  7. Meritocracy of ideas.
  8. Lead with context, not control.
  9. A higher purpose.
  10. A self-building machine powered by feedback cycles.
- **Recruiting (Bilbao): "talent is infinite"** ("el talento es infinito"). If you can't find people, you aren't searching hard enough. Where to look:
  - Universities: entrepreneurship clubs and professors.
  - Talent arbitrage: salespeople from tough sales models, consultancies, startups that are shutting down.
  - Partnerships with creators.
- **Profile to look for:** high agency, persuasive, intellectually curious, learns on their own. Prefer people who have already done the thing over people who promise they will.
- **Freelancers to in-house:** 30X started with freelancers and is now moving the whole team in-house.

## Numbers & rules of thumb

- 6 core skills on a social team: strategy, recording, editing, distribution/content management, metrics, design. AI cuts across all of them.
- Ideal squad: about 5 people. Beyond 5 it starts getting chaotic, so add an AI person only if needed.
- A ~6-person team out-produced a 70-person communications team.
- One person grew an account from 0 to ~1.7M followers alone, at ~1 video/day.
- ~99% of people start alone or with one content person.
- The instructor's company advisory program runs 4 months. In ~99% of cases he doesn't ask the company to hire, at most to duplicate a key person so knowledge isn't lost.
- Planning: ~10 minutes to define the week's 7 videos (at 1 video/day).
- Review meetings: daily 10 min (if volume is high) and weekly 30–60 min.
- Experiments: about 1 in 10 videos. Keep 80–90% for your proven format and use the rest to experiment.
- At 2 videos/day, that's ~8 experiments/month (figure as stated in class).
- At one video per hour (campaign pace), the team needed 2 videographers and 2 editors. With an AI editing pipeline, one editor now covers the work of two.
- A right hand who "duplicates" the creator took ~9 months to learn the creator's taste.
- Example of the routine bottleneck: posting only ~3 videos/week.

## Frameworks / step-by-step methods

**Hiring by bottleneck**
1. Name today's bottleneck: routine, tasks you hate, connection, quality, volume, or a pipeline stage (recording, editing, analysis, coordination).
2. Decide whether you can solve it yourself (skills are learnable) or need a hire, and whether you can afford it.
3. Hire for that single problem, preferably one person who covers several skills.
4. Document what the role does so it can later be automated or handed to the next hire.
5. Measure the impact: videos/week, followers, leads.
6. Repeat with the next bottleneck.

**Team growth path**
1. Solo: the creator covers all six skills, using AI.
2. First hire: a videographer-editor in one person, or a right hand.
3. As volume saturates, split videographer and editor and add a content manager.
4. Scale to the ~5-person squad: strategist, content manager, videographer, editor, designer. Add an AI specialist only if needed.

**Weekly operating cadence**
1. Plan the week's videos quickly (~10 min).
2. Spend the time producing.
3. Review: daily 10 min when volume is high, weekly 30–60 min on what worked, what didn't and next week's plan.
4. Keep ~90% on the proven format and ~10% on experiments.

## Pitfalls / what instructors warned against

- Hiring by job title or toward an "ideal team" instead of today's bottleneck.
- Assuming more people means more speed or quality. Large teams decide slowly.
- Hiring a cinema-grade videographer when what you need is speed and flexibility.
- Hiring senior, directive profiles who have never made content.
- Hiring anyone weak at AI.
- Spending time over-planning instead of producing and reviewing.
- Abandoning a working format, or never experimenting at all.
- Treating slow growth as a "shadowban" when the content isn't good enough yet. Volume and format fixes come first.
- Building the early team cheaply on the belief that "anyone will do" while bootstrapping. Bilbao said this forced him to replace people one by one, and he puts talent density first.
- If fear stops you from posting, make a public commitment (e.g. "100 posts") to create pressure.
- Open question: Bilbao said he has no clear answer for building culture in fully remote teams.
