# Session 3 — How social platforms and the algorithm work (Sesión 3 — Cómo funcionan las redes y el algoritmo)

Problem: content gets made without knowing why some videos reach millions and others die at a few hundred views → the algorithm rewards watch time, so every piece must be actionable, hooked in the first 2 seconds, explained simply, and copied from formats that already proved themselves.

## Key ideas
- **The algorithm is an ally, not an enemy.** Instagram/TikTok/Facebook sell attention: more time on platform = more ads shown = more revenue. The algorithm surfaces whatever keeps people watching. Read every metric through that lens. This is the part that never changes when "the algorithm changes".
- **Watch time beats "interaction".** The instructor prefers to reason in retention, not likes/comments/follows.
- **The "2X" standard:** aim for people to watch the video twice, fully. Example: one video had 23M plays on 15M accounts reached, so roughly 8M plays were repeat views.
- **Each classic metric maps to watch time:**
  - Share: brings a new user into the platform. Strongest signal. Driven by identification + value.
  - Save: promises a future re-watch. Requires real value.
  - Comment: the video keeps looping while the person types (another "2X").
  - Like: validates that the content was liked, not just noticed.
- **Distribution works as a series of filters** (rough model): ~10% of your own audience sees it first → if they interact, ~50% of your audience → if they interact again, it goes out to non-followers. Implication: a bot-filled audience never passes filter 1, however good the video is.
- **Account health comes before strategy.** Growth blockers, in order: (1) purchased followers/bots (worst; account is effectively lost), (2) growth only through ads (not lost, but the audience must be "refreshed"), (3) a few past viral hits followed by unrelated formats (natural, not a penalty).
- **We are in the post-influencer era:** having 100K followers no longer guarantees reach. A good video works, a bad one doesn't. Reach drops come from audiences tiring of a repeated stimulus, not from a "shadowban".
- **Three content types, as a pyramid/funnel:**
  - Sales content (talks about the product directly): low reach.
  - Value content: useful, but tends to become too technical.
  - Niche-viral content: not dancing or humor. Simple content that reaches wide *within the same sector*.
  - The widest-reaching content in an account sets the reach ceiling for everything else. In organic, people must first *discover* you before value and sales content can work.
- **Organic has no forced objective, unlike ads.** You choose the call to action: follow (growth), comment (enters a funnel: comment → link/DM → qualification), or nothing (reach only).
- **Traffic is useless if the profile isn't optimized.** Prepare the profile first, then drive people to it.
- **Topic selection:** start from who you are talking to and what interests them, not only what you sell. Example: to reach business owners, talk leadership, teams, sales (broad), not only the specific service.
- **"Value" has a strict operational definition:** the viewer can apply it right after the video. Motivational, generic, or insider-only advice doesn't count. Content must be either actionable or highly revealing.
- **"If you want X, do Y" formats work** because the viewer feels they just solved something.
- **Hook = context, not delivery.** In the first 2 seconds the viewer must know (or feel) what they'll get, and build a mental picture of what's coming. That anticipation is what holds them. Don't deliver the payload up front. Give context and leave a loop open.
- **Strong hook tactic: challenge a common belief.** Pattern: "Why the rich say you should NOT buy the house you live in." Others: "3 films that are a sales university", "3 careers I don't recommend studying", "When someone interrupts you, say this".
- **Classic list hooks ("3 mistakes you shouldn't make") are no longer enough** on their own.
- **Simplicity over sophistication.** Example: "use a different frame every 2,000 ms to raise retention" vs "to go viral, change scene every 2 seconds". Same information, opposite performance. Viewers typically grasp only 30–60% of what you meant, and less if they don't finish. Experts who communicate well say things simply. You compete against entertainment.
- **Show, don't just tell.** Dramatize, use body language, or put supporting footage on top while a person talks on the bottom, so the viewer doesn't have to imagine it.
- **Stories over data:** stories activate 8–9 brain areas vs 2–3 for logic/metrics. Classic structure: hook → story → offer.
- **The format is the "how it is taught":** whiteboard ("Need X? Use Y"), categorization (e.g. "train vs fire" for employee behaviors), ranked list 1–10, text overlay below the speaker, good-tools vs bad-tools.
- **Replicate what worked, literally.** Re-recording a winning video has ~90% odds of working again (same or different background is fine). Iterate only once something has already worked. Next level of variation: keep the exact narrative but swap the inner content (same "3 leadership tips" frame, different tips).
- **When testing a borrowed format, copy the structure exactly.** Asking an AI for "ideas based on this format" slows down learning whether the format works for you. Same language and same country: don't clone it word for word. Foreign-language source (English/Portuguese): you can translate it with the same structure.
- **Niche growth pattern:** new niches grow by importing formats that already worked in other niches (e.g. fitness/nutrition → business/leadership). No viral content in your niche means opportunity, not a dead end.
- **Evergreen vs expiring formats:** some formats wear out, others last for years. Track both. Psychology doesn't change.
- **Experience/tour niches (real estate, premium services):** the best-converting piece is a continuous, linear walkthrough with someone narrating, so the viewer feels inside it. No jump cuts from facade to living room. Even if it doesn't go viral, it attracts highly interested buyers. Pair it with broad-topic content to attract an audience.
- **Content that converts best today: lead magnets/resources delivered via comments.** Then measure which communication angles bring *qualified* leads (e.g. if talking about "delegating" attracts better clients, do more of it). Two separate scorecards: leads generated vs growth/interaction generated.
- **Monetizing organic via profile:**
  - Profile photo and name give context.
  - Bio in 3 parts: (1) whom you help and with what (specific), (2) why listen to you (years, clients, results), (3) a direction/CTA ("DM us", "click below").
  - Link in bio is the first but least scalable method. Comment-triggered funnels are the most scalable.
  - A story highlight should explain whom you help, with what, and whom you *don't* help.
  - A welcome automation for new followers opens DM conversations that become prospects.
- **Ads vs organic:** putting ads on content changes its objective to direct return. First validate in organic which content sells, then put spend behind that. Don't stop ads that are bringing sales until something else works.
- **Faceless accounts can grow** (especially with carousels above ~5K followers), but a human face humanizes the brand and connects more. It doesn't have to always be the same person.
- **Execution beats planning:** "Vale más el feedback que vas a tener que la idea" (the feedback you'll get is worth more than the idea). Launch several versions of a first video and keep the one that works. If a life event delays your "real" account, start practicing on a hobby account now.
- **Volume only helps with strategy and quality.** One creator with 1,600 videos was stuck at 20K followers. With fewer than 200 strategic videos she reached 600K.
- **Cross-posting:** a short video costs nothing extra to publish on Instagram, TikTok, Facebook and YouTube. Example: one video got 22M on TikTok, 21M on Instagram and 3M on Facebook. Some accounts do better on Instagram than TikTok. Follow your own data.

## Numbers & rules of thumb
- Algorithm filter model: ~10% of own audience → ~50% of own audience → non-followers.
- 2X rule: target 2 full views per viewer. Example: 23M plays / 15M reached ≈ 8M repeat views.
- >80% of platform content is now video.
- Hook window: under 2 seconds to break the scroll pattern. Viewer should know/feel the payoff within the first 2 s.
- 60/10 rule: spend 60% of production time on the first 10 seconds (ideally the first 5).
- Hook-only change case: same video republished 7 times, changing only the hook: 10K views on the first try → 45M on the 7th.
- Comprehension: viewers typically understand 30–60% of what you intended (and less without a full watch).
- Stories activate 8–9 brain zones vs 2–3 for data/logic.
- Re-recording a winning video: ~90% probability it works again.
- Format by account size: Reels 100% until ~4,000–5,000 followers. Carousels work after ~5,000 (including ad-grown accounts). Feed posts on a new account only reach existing followers.
- Viral benchmark for *your own* content: >10K views = above typical. 50K–100K+ views = viral.
- Viral benchmark for *reference* content to copy: at least 15,000–20,000 likes. Judge by likes, not views (views can include paid reach). Example: 1M views with 1K likes is not organic. Caveat: desktop may show fewer likes than mobile, because mobile adds Facebook likes.
- Healthy small account example: ~200 followers with a 3K-view reel and 50–100+ likes = account in good state.
- Unhealthy signal: e.g. ~50K followers averaging 1–2K views and only 7–20 likes → check for bots.
- New-account decision: an ad-grown account under ~2,000 followers that wants big growth → start fresh. 2K–10K followers can be overtaken quickly with good structure.
- Trial reels: publish for at least 1 week (ideally 1–2 weeks, daily content) before deciding to open a new account.
- Video length: about 24–40 s, up to 1 minute. Avoid ultra-short 2–3 s clips (views without impact). Longer only with proven formats.
- Batch production: plan 1 week / 15 days / 1 month of content in a single session (e.g. 15 pieces for 2 weeks).
- Instructor track record cited: ~80 videos to grow a new TikTok. Accounts reaching 500K followers with <50 posts and 180K with 16 posts. A client account reached 700K IG / 2M FB in ~130 posts (~4 months). Viral videos at 26M views (+~90K followers), 50M views, and single videos bringing 100K–170K followers. 13M accounts reached with >99.8% non-followers. Sales vs value example: 1K vs 6M organic views.
- Carousel-only growth example: a client went from 500K to 1M in 2 months with carousels only.
- Old hashtag myth: 30 hashtags per post didn't help (5 followers after 10 perfect posts).

## Frameworks / step-by-step methods
**"5 steps to make a business brand reach 1M potential clients"** (only steps 1–3 were fully covered. Steps 4–5 were compressed for time):
1. **Content is king:** pick an idea that is actionable and revealing. Score every piece you've published: can the viewer apply it immediately? How revealing is it, 1–10?
2. **Hook:** state the biggest problem or solution up front. Give context, don't deliver. Optionally challenge a common belief.
3. **Attractive delivery:** simple language, dramatization, stories, visual support, a recognizable format (whiteboard, categorization, list).
4. *(compressed)* Production basics: 24–40 s, a clip-on mic (wireless lavalier type, not unstable foam-topped vertical mics), clear vocalization, good lighting. Audio determines how much of the message is understood.
5. *(compressed)* Distribute and repeat: post the same video across platforms, and re-record winners.

**Winning-format loop:**
1. Find what already works (others' formats, other niches, other languages).
2. Apply it with the exact structure.
3. Identify which applied format performed best for you. That is your winner.
4. Make more of it (re-record, then narrative-preserving variations).
5. Never stop testing outside proven formats too, to keep the view average up.

**Account diagnosis (before strategy):**
1. Were followers/bots ever bought? If yes → new account.
2. Did it grow only via ads? → refresh the audience with trial reels.
3. Is engagement proportional to followers (views/likes vs follower count)?
4. Post trial reels following proven structures for 1–2 weeks (they don't show on the profile but are distributed in the Reels feed, like "a new account without creating one"). Also try carousels if over ~5K.
5. If neither trial reels nor carousels move the average → open a new account.

**New account setup:** make it clearly different from the old one (photo, bio) so it isn't treated as a duplicate. Post only structured, reference-based Reels. No sales content and no feed posts at the start. The goal is to beat the average of other new accounts.

**TikTok as lab:** when running *variations*, test on TikTok first and move winners to Instagram. When following *exact proven references*, post on both. Archive what underperforms on Instagram if the view average worries you.

**Keyword research for references:** list seed keywords for your niche → expand them with an AI assistant → search each one in Instagram search, also in English and Portuguese. One keyword may return nothing, another a whole world of winners.

**Homework method:** while consuming content, study *how* it's taught (the format), not *what* is taught.

## Pitfalls / what instructors warned against
- Buying followers/bots: the worst thing you can do. The account can't pass the first distribution filter.
- Making only sales content in organic. People must discover you first. Don't post sales content on a new account.
- Believing the myths: best editing/production, AI-written scripts, the perfect posting hour, the best hashtags, a perfect feed grid, the best camera, or humor as the only way to reach people.
- Spending weeks crafting a "perfect launch" (10 polished posts, perfect bio, 30 hashtags). It didn't work.
- Volume without strategy (1,600 videos → 20K followers).
- Weak or generic hooks. Front-loading a generic promise ("I'll teach you 10 marketing tools") into a proven format kills its performance.
- Hooks that give no context: the viewer must picture what's coming.
- Technical jargon and sophistication. Motivational or generic "value" that isn't applicable.
- Paraphrasing or AI-remixing a reference before validating the original format: it slows down learning. Don't clone same-language, same-country videos verbatim.
- Iterating hooks on content that hasn't worked yet. Iterate only after a hit.
- Driving traffic to an unoptimized profile.
- Paying to boost follower counts (ad-bought followers) instead of earning them through organic interaction.
- Real-estate/tour videos with disconnected jump cuts.
- Bad audio: a great message with poor sound isn't understood.
- Stopping profitable ad campaigns before organic is proven.
- Overthinking and waiting to launch. Execution and feedback are worth more than the plan.
