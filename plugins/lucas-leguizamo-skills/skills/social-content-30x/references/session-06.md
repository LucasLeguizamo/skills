# Session 6 — The TikTok Machine and Content Series (Sesión 6 — La máquina de TikTok y las series de contenido)

Problem: creators copy formats endlessly, lose identity, post on one network and expect results in two weeks → Answer: build a system (Universe → Pillars → Series), use TikTok as the lab and Instagram as the showcase, replicate everywhere, add content sources, and let volume raise the odds of virality.

## Key ideas
- **Hierarchy: Universe → Pillars → Series.** Universe = everything you could talk about (experience, tastes, hobbies, obsessions, people around you, clients, personality, stories). Pillars = the strategic subset that serves your goal. Series = a concrete format you repeat inside a pillar. Confusing universe with pillars is the common mistake.
- **Start from the goal.** Before formats, be clear on why you create (bring clients, enter a market, etc.), ideally a long-term goal, and ask constantly: "what do I want people to think of me when they hear me?"
- **"You are much more than your job"** (personal brands and company brands alike). Talking only about your trade leaves nothing to differentiate you or connect on. Each universe element is a separate entry door: people arrive emotionally (shared parenthood, a hobby, a side interest such as AI) and stay for the core content.
- **Copying formats is the starting mechanism, identity is the long-term asset.** The mix of the formats you copy becomes your identity only if you inject your universe into them. Pure copying makes it hard for anyone to know who you are; a creator who blends their people, stories and personality into copied formats doesn't look like a copier.
- **Opening your life builds trust.** Viewers feel they "know" a creator whose family, partner and side projects appear in content, and it is easier to do business with someone who opened up. Optional: if you don't want to show family, that part of the universe is simply off-limits.
- **Pillars are discovered by publishing, not planned at a table.** The instructor planned to be entertaining (as with friends) and discovered on camera he can't do humor but can teach.
- **Pillars are guides, not rules.** The best creators break their own rules; post something off-pillar if you feel like it and evaluate after.
- **Series: find a format that works and squeeze it to the end.** Don't reinvent the wheel every video; change hook/elements while keeping the structure. Some accounts run on one pillar + one format for years (e.g. a daily "friend's advice" walk-and-talk; a creator who stands in front of luxury-brand logos and riffs on their branding; a private-jet broker who films live client calls).
- **Within a series the breakout often comes late** — of ~20 videos the hit may be #12 or #13; keep replicating what averages well.
- **Series life cycle:** copy → works → normalizes → decays. Two exits on decline: reinvent (add new dynamics) or already have another series running in parallel. Creators who "blame the algorithm" are usually doing exactly the same thing as before.
- **TikTok is the lab, Instagram the showcase.** TikTok is the "purest" algorithm: followers don't matter (1M followers + bad video ≈ 300–400 views; 0 followers + good video can hit 30M views in 2 days). Instagram "engaña" (deceives): the community props up likes even on bad videos, so you don't learn. Brands also feel less inhibited experimenting on TikTok. Bring to Instagram only what already won on TikTok; keep Instagram curated.
- **TikTok is the hardest network, so aim to win there** — if you get good on TikTok, the rest follows. Still, if a different direction (Meta first) works for you, it is valid; no universal rule.
- **Distribution across 5 platforms: replicate first, personalize later.** More distribution = more chances to find audience and more data. Over time you discover per-network differences (e.g. the instructor's car-recorded TikTok format performs terribly on Instagram).
- **Hits usually travel:** something that hits hard on TikTok probably works on Instagram, Facebook and YouTube, but not always both ways.
- **Multiply content sources.** The daily on-camera video stays the main growth lever, but doesn't scale alone. Add podcasts (own or guesting), interviews, webinars, classes, long-form YouTube, lives, people in your universe. Large accounts reach very high daily output this way, not by sitting down to record each video.
- **"I don't seek virality, I increase the odds that it exists."** Build a system with enough volume for virality to appear, then steer it to the business goal.
- **Virality can hurt.** A video about working for free went viral among the wrong audience and damaged the brand. Views ≠ business metric. If a viral video brings mostly hate: admit the mistake, learn, don't repeat.
- **Polemic comments on topic are good.** In ~50 s you can't cover every angle; people add angles → conversation. Mine the most repeated objection and make it the next video; the community feels you "read their minds."
- **Long form converts better.** Short-form gives a viewer minutes of you in total; a 1-hour live or interview builds much more trust. Once short form is dominated, move to long form (YouTube or streaming). Instructor's bet: in ~4 years AI content will be indistinguishable, so live (streaming) becomes proof of real human content.
- **Selling content:** both indirect selling (keep giving value, mention the product mid-story) and fully sales-built videos work; test both. The subtler it feels, the more the viewer enjoys being sold to. Paid ads can run in parallel to organic if you want to sell.
- **Trends vs viral formats:** prefer copying viral formats over chasing trends, unless you genuinely love trends. Most companies err by joining trends for the sake of it; drop a trend if it pulls the account toward an off-target audience.
- **Industries where competitors don't make content:** don't copy competitors; copy creators in the world of your product (the universe "upstream" of the product can be infinite).
- **Feed aesthetics matter less than content.** Good content wins even if the grid is ugly; a top designer can't save bad content. Do pin a few videos with nice covers; build recognizability with recurring elements (same background, same shirt, same place).
- **Compare yourself to yourself, not to the reference.** The creator you copy speaks better on camera and edits better; judge progress month over month against your own past.
- **Profile optimization:** keywords in the profile matching what you teach, videos on that topic, account public. No hidden "settings" boost reach.

## Numbers & rules of thumb
- Pillars: 1 very strong pillar can work alone; 2–3 is ideal; maximum ~3–4. More than 3 makes the account hard to identify.
- 90/10 rule: 90% of content under your rules (pillars/series), 10% pure experimentation.
- TikTok: 1M followers + bad video ≈ 300–400 views; 0 followers + good video can reach 30M views in 2 days.
- 100 videos on TikTok teach far more than 100 on Instagram.
- The instructor has uploaded ~30 videos in a day to TikTok and replicated the same video up to ~10 times with the same hook without penalty; don't fear posting one video ~6 times with different hooks.
- Podcast math: 60-min podcast → ~14 good clips → ~5 work → ~3 break out → make a second version of those 3 (new hook, longer, transitions) → ~25–30 videos total, ~7 viral, from one recording. Some podcasts yield 7–8 viral clips; typically ~3.
- Scale volume gradually (1 → 2 → 3 → 4 a day), not 1 → 25 overnight.
- One video a day is already hard; it's the sacred baseline. Push to 2–4/day if possible.
- 2 weeks of posting = ~14 videos (28 for the most diligent): too little data to judge anything.
- The 100-video milestone: clarity arrives at video 100, more at 200 and 300. At 3 videos/day you reach ~30 videos in ~9–10 days and 100 in about a month.
- Daily posting without viral formats beats one copied viral video per week.
- Evaluate progress in monthly periods, not 2.5 weeks.
- Hate ratio heuristic: 10,000 likes with 10,000 hate comments = you messed up; 10,000 likes with ~30 comments of which ~10 are hate = normal. With 1M viewers, even 1% haters is thousands of people, and haters comment more than fans.
- Short-form exposure: e.g. 10 videos of 30 s ≈ at most ~5 min with you vs 1 hour in a live/interview.
- Pin ~3 videos with designed covers on the profile.
- Carousels: participants reported 4–10 slides working better than 20; instructor: if a 20-slide viral carousel exists in your niche, copy it at 20.
- Claim raised in class by a TikTok Shop seller (not the instructor's own data; instructor accepted it): to rank a TikTok Shop account, ~100 videos/week plus at least 3 lives/week of ≥1 continuous hour; videos over ~40 s lose traction; TikTok penalizes slow customer responses and misleading claims via an account score; a push of ~30 videos in 4 days coincided with doubled weekly sales.
- Hiring a strong YouTube advisor: expect ~1 year to become good at YouTube.
- Product sellers: commit ~3 months of concentrated TikTok testing.
- Session homework: at least 15 more videos in 15 days.

## Frameworks / step-by-step methods
**A. Building identity and pillars**
1. Define the long-term goal of creating content and what you want people to think of you.
2. Map your universe: experiences/learnings, likes, passions, hobbies, obsessions, people (family, team, clients), personality, stories.
3. While copying formats and publishing, filter candidate pillars with four tests: (a) does it serve my goal? (b) does it come from my real experience? (c) does it reinforce what I want to be known for? (d) is it working?
4. If something works but doesn't serve the goal, re-angle that topic toward the goal rather than starting a new pillar from zero.
5. Keep 1–3 (max 4) pillars; leave 10% for wildcard experiments.

**B. Running a series**
1. Copy a format that already went viral.
2. If it works, repeat it changing hook/elements, not structure.
3. Keep replicating what averages well; expect the hit around video 10–15 of a run.
4. Batch it (e.g. ask 20 true/false questions in one recording session to cut several videos).
5. On decline: reinvent with new dynamics, or switch to a parallel series already being tested.

**C. Multiplatform machine**
1. Step 1 — replicate: post each Reel as-is on TikTok, Instagram, Facebook (Instagram's share-to-Facebook toggle), YouTube Shorts; for personal brands paste the script as a LinkedIn text post (or test the video).
2. Step 2 — personalize over time: learn what each network rewards and adapt formats; long-term, someone reviews each network.
3. Volume mode: put everything on TikTok → move TikTok's best to Instagram → replicate Instagram to the rest.
4. Funnel for larger operations: goal → universe → pillars that work → proven series → many sources (podcast, YouTube, interviews, lives, talking head, long recordings cut into clips) → distribute everywhere → read results per network → create again.
5. Treat clips with judgment: they don't come packaged in viral formats; dumping them through AI without thought gives bad results. Learn what makes a clip work and do more of it.

**D. Carousels** — same as Reels: find one that worked, study its structure, rebuild with your identity (photo, typography, colors), publish, observe, replicate.

## Tools mentioned
- **CapCut** — the only editor needed; pay for the paid version if you can ("best investment in the world"); free version has fewer fonts and ads. Learn by doing and searching specific how-tos when stuck.
- **Instagram "share to Facebook"** toggle for replication; **Instagram trial reels** — shown to non-followers; useful for large accounts to test reach with strangers; small accounts can simply post as main.
- **Claude / any AI** — to extract a transcript/script from a reference video.
- **ManyChat** — comment-triggered auto-replies (covered in the sales session).
- **TikTok Shop**, **TikTok Live**, **YouTube**, **Spotify** (podcasts; harder to grow without connections — get good on YouTube first), **Substack** (instructor's long-form destination linked from LinkedIn posts).

## Pitfalls / what instructors warned against
- Using planning ("I'll publish once X is figured out") as an excuse not to publish; publishing is the only thing that works.
- Expecting results in 2–3 weeks; "nadie se vuelve un referente en redes en un par de semanas" (nobody becomes a reference in a couple of weeks).
- Copying forever without adding identity.
- Too many pillars (>3–4): audience no longer knows what you are.
- Talking only about your job.
- Judging learning on Instagram, where community likes mask bad videos.
- Repeating the same thing and blaming the algorithm when a series decays.
- Running clips through AI with no judgment.
- Chasing views as the metric; virality among the wrong audience can damage the brand.
- Chasing trends instead of copying viral formats.
- Comparing your numbers with the reference creator's.
- Authority videos when you aren't yet an authority — they feel fake; do the formats that feel natural and get reach.
- "Comment X and I'll send you the guide" is a selling/email-capture mechanism, not a growth mechanism.
- Myths debunked: native-vs-external carousel design, posting hour and editing app don't determine performance — content quality does. You don't need to show your face to start (thousands of faceless formats; voice-over works).
- Starting a YouTube channel alone from zero; better to pay an expert.
- Influencers who stop iterating once comfortable lose relevance; the core skill is iterating and adapting fast, and "it never gets solved."
- Feed-aesthetic obsession over content volume.
