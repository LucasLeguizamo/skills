# Session 5 — Viral Format System (Sesión 5 — Sistema de formatos virales)

Problem: creators invent content from scratch, guess, and burn volume without learning anything → Answer: find videos that already went viral organically in your niche, deconstruct them into concrete variables, replicate the structure (keeping the hook), measure, then milk your own winners.

## Key ideas

- **"Growth Viral Hacking": don't invent, deconstruct.** Successful businesses don't do something new, they do what already exists *better*. Instagram itself copied DMs (Messenger), Stories (Snapchat) and Reels (TikTok); the platforms even tell creators to "post your version of another creator's content". The market (= the algorithm = watch time) decides what works.
- **Three sources of format ideas:** (1) exploit what already worked for you, (2) winning formats in your niche in other languages (Spanish, English, Portuguese), (3) formats that repeat across *other* niches (Top-5 lists, "this helps you gain / maintain / lose" three-category boards, etc.) adapted to yours.
- **Search keywords by the solution, not your product.** If the niche word returns nothing viral, switch keywords; use angles your audience cares about (e.g., for B2B: sales, team, operations, marketing). Ask an AI to expand a seed list of keywords. If a broad profession word only surfaces comedy/entertainment, use a more specific keyword instead of turning to humor.
- **Organic vs paid filter:** a reference must have strong *likes*, not just views. Huge views with few likes means ads pushed it — people watched but didn't interact.
- **Pick references you can actually execute:** recent, replicable location, actionable. Skip celebrity podcasts/conference clips (situation not replicable) and pure motivational/inspirational content.
- **Main idea first:** state in one sentence what the viewer takes away and what they can *do* after watching (e.g., a 4-question "is this a dumb expense?" checklist; "don't say X, say Y"; "foods good vs bad for weight loss").
- **"Lo que menos hay que cambiar es el gancho"** (the hook is what you change least). Copy the hook almost literally. Changing the hook is the most common reason people think "the format is burned" when they never actually followed it.
- **Some ideas are evergreen**, not just the format: e.g., "a million dollars today or a penny doubled daily for 30 days?" works year after year. A creator not having exhausted a format doesn't mean it can't be exhausted further — that's the opportunity.
- **A video = scenes; a scene = sum of variables** (idea, location, camera, light, protagonist, audio, body language, tone). Identify them all in the reference and the only thing left is volume.
- **Camera position and safe zone:** distance changes the message (1 m vs 10 cm). Leave room at the top for the title and at the bottom for the caption; Instagram crops edges, so keep subtitles away from borders. Don't frame yourself only in the bottom half, leaving the top empty.
- **Lighting:** light in front, never backlit (window behind you), clean the lens before every take. A ring light / key light plus a background lamp is enough; a pro camera is not required (a creator grew to 2M filmed by family on a phone).
- **Audio is the % of the message understood.** Recording with earbuds, phone mic or in noise can sink a good video. Volume too low is worse than too high; compare against the reference. Sound effects/music must never be louder than the voice.
- **Pace matters:** if the reference is 1 min and your read of the same script is 2 min, you doubled the structure. Use hands and vary intonation to avoid sounding robotic; emphasis changes meaning.
- **Editing is less important than everything before recording.** Fancy effects and fonts are not scalable. What matters: cut silences, simple subtitles, voice on top.
- **The "millennial pause" kills videos:** the breath between hitting record and speaking makes most viewers swipe. Cut silence at the start, the end and between phrases — the single most important edit.
- **Subtitles always**, even if the reference has none (many watch muted; audio + text + visuals = more senses, more retention). Plain style: no border, no colored background boxes; light shadow is fine.
- **External/B-roll clips** (demo video on top, speaker below): find them via TikTok (including its image-similarity search) or reuse the reference's own clips. Download without watermark and in HD — watermarks and low quality cut reach. Using the same imagery the reference used generally avoids rights issues.
- **Two content types, two metrics:** audience content → measured in followers, needs a "follow me" CTA. Lead content → "comment WORD and I'll send you the resource" (automated DM), measured by comment volume and *quality* of leads; track which topics bring better leads and double down (works especially well in carousels).
- **Many views, few followers** is usually a missing CTA (or a too-short 5–10 s clip), not bad content. Organic needs explicit direction, unlike ads. "Sígueme para más contenido" at the end already lifts follow conversion.
- **Bio in 3 parts:** who you help and with what → authority in numbers (clients, years) → CTA ("start here" / "message me"). Add a pinned highlight that introduces you and states who you do *not* help (filtering raises conversion). Your landing page should carry the same promise.
- **Covers:** the in-feed first frame (the hook, where most discovery happens) matters more than the profile-grid cover. Give context with a 2-phrase title saying what the viewer gets. Simplest cover: a frame of the video + title, aligned with the brand.
- **Repurposing a winner:** transcribe the script, ask an AI "give me exactly part 2 without changing anything", re-record. Same for cross-niche formats: transcribe, translate, ask the AI to adapt it to your niche (feed it your past winning scripts too).
- **Vary formats** even while exploiting a winner, so you aren't stranded when one stops working. Volume never has negative consequences.
- **Virality is non-linear:** one hits, one doesn't, the next hits. Live with uncertainty; a format proves itself published, not in your head ("Uno sabe que un formato es ganador publicado, no en la cabeza"). If it fails, it's feedback.
- **Ads use the same logic** (organic algorithm increasingly resembles ads: more watch time → lower CPM). Winning organic formats often become winning ads. But in ads, speak to the avatar from the first seconds (filters who sees it) and measure by lead quality, not views. Don't pay to grow audience; if ads dominate an account, organic gets harder later — keep posting organic.
- **Reels still work** ("Reels are dead, carousels win" is a polarizing marketing angle, not a fact). Carousels are shown first to your existing audience, so small accounts should start with Reels. The "hopeful polarity" angle itself ("you're not failing because of you, it's because of X") is a strong communication device to reuse.
- **Captions, hashtags, music, posting time are minor.** Caption = extend the info (except "read the caption" micro-videos). In-video spoken words and subtitles feed search/SEO more than the caption. Choosing trending audio no longer makes a video viral.
- **Property/listing niches (example of niche-specific formats):** pair community content (e.g., "pipe breaks — tenant or owner pays?") with full uninterrupted walkthroughs from the façade, no jump cuts between rooms (speed up instead), stating location, area and price to filter by geography.
- **Background doesn't matter** for value content (except context niches like gym exercises). Keep production simple so you don't procrastinate.

## Numbers & rules of thumb

- Organic reference filter: minimum 20,000–30,000 likes (aim for 30k).
- Paid-content tell: ~1,000,000 views with only ~1,000–5,000 likes.
- Reference age: less than 1 year old.
- Working duration range: 15 s to 1 min 30 s (important but not the top factor). Exception: if your 1.5-min+ videos already work, keep doing them.
- Short 5–10 s clips can rack up views (e.g., 6M) but tend to bring few followers.
- Communication split cited: words 7%, voice 38%, body language 55%.
- Judge a post after at least 7 days, never in one day; carousels can take off ~2 weeks later.
- Definitive failure: below ~500 views → archive (after re-checking reference quality, execution, account health, and a trial-reel retry).
- Success benchmark vs a reference: beating your own account's usual views, not matching the reference's number.
- Penalized-account signals: bought bots ever; ~5,000 views with 10–20 likes (fewer than ~30 likes); persistent ceiling of 20,000–30,000 views despite correct strategy.
- New account threshold: under 10,000–20,000 followers, starting fresh is often better.
- Penalized-account protocol: publish ~14 videos as trial reels following references; if none beats the account's max, try carousels; if still nothing, new profile.
- New account rules: different email (and number), different photo, different bio; ideally another phone; don't follow your old network (non-niche followers hurt interaction).
- Reposting old content on a new account: not while growing; only after >10,000 followers, max 1 per week, only past winners, re-edited (slightly sped up without distorting audio) so it isn't detected as duplicate.
- Sales vs audience mix: new accounts focus on audience first (an account under ~10k that starts with sales content grows poorly); afterwards run both.
- Hashtags: limit dropped from 30 to 3 → low relevance; spend under a minute on them.
- Posting time: followers usually most active 3–6 pm; posting ~5 pm is fine.
- Foreign-narrative markets (e.g., very different storytelling cultures): at most ~10% of your references; less predictable.
- Lead automation ("comment WORD") takes ~5 minutes to configure; can require "follow first" before sending.
- Cadence: 5–7 pieces per week; batch-record weekly or every 2 weeks with transcribed scripts on a teleprompter.
- Cases cited: 1,700 posts stuck under 20k followers → under 100 strategic videos → 600k+; a new profile with ~40 posts → ~300k; another with ~30 videos → ~300k; brands growing 600k in 4 months; ~2M via Reels across IG/TikTok/Facebook; same video 20M on IG/TikTok vs 3M on Facebook; 80+ success cases.
- In ~90% of cases where "there's no content in my niche", there is — research is just the slow part.

## Frameworks / step-by-step methods

**A. Find a reference (Growth Viral Hacking)**
1. Open Instagram search (or web version / TikTok search if your app version hides view counts) and enter a niche keyword in ES, EN or PT.
2. Scan views (bottom-left) and likes; keep only videos with 20–30k+ likes, under 1 year old.
3. Discard celebrity podcast/conference clips, pure motivation, and anything you can't replicate (exotic locations).
4. Prefer actionable content; pick one you can film this week.

**B. Deconstruction checklist (per reference)**
1. Main idea in one sentence + what the viewer can do afterward.
2. Duration.
3. Location / context.
4. Camera position and distance; safe zones for title (top) and caption (bottom).
5. Lighting.
6. Protagonist(s) and props (e.g., whiteboard must be legible).
7. What is said and done: tone, speed, gestures, body language.
8. Audio quality and volume.
9. Editing: scene joins, external clips, silences, subtitles, sound effects.
→ Variables form a scene; scenes form the video. Replicate, keeping the hook nearly verbatim.

**C. When a video flops — diagnose in order**
1. Was the reference really good (interaction filter)?
2. Did you follow it correctly (hook unchanged, framing, pace, audio)?
3. Is the account healthy (no bots, not grown only via ads, fails on all platforms)?
4. After 7 days, republish as a trial reel; if still under ~500 views, archive.

**D. When a video works — exploit it**
1. Transcribe the script.
2. Ask an AI for "exactly part 2, change nothing"; re-record with the same hook and settings.
3. Apply the same script to adjacent topics (same structure, different app/tool/subject).
4. Keep a log: reference → your published video → followers gained; scale what wins.

**E. Research account that trains the algorithm**
1. Create a new account used only for research (never for posting).
2. Search the niche in 3 languages; follow and interact with good references.
3. Watch good videos to the end — saving and skipping tells the algorithm "this didn't retain me".
4. Don't linger on bad content. Scroll the feed when stuck; new references appear.
5. Instagram's "Inspiration" panel in insights is another source.

**F. Production routine**
1. Schedule a recording slot today or tomorrow for the reference you picked.
2. Transcribe scripts, read them on a teleprompter app.
3. Edit simply: cut silences, plain subtitles, voice above effects. An editor can come later; don't let editing block publishing.

- Instagram search / web Instagram; TikTok search (incl. image-similarity search) — research. Instagram Trial Reels; Instagram insights "Inspiration" section; content-preferences reset (less reliable than a fresh account).
- ManyChat — "comment WORD" DM automation (no price given).
- CapCut — cutting silences, subtitles.
- SnapTik, SaveFrom — download TikTok clips without watermark (download in HD).
- Claude (AI) — keyword expansion, transcribe/translate/adapt formats, "part 2" scripts.

- Ring light / front light, background lamp.

## Pitfalls / what instructors warned against

- Changing the hook when adapting a reference.
- Using paid-looking references (high views, low likes) as proof of a format.
- Concluding after one day; abandoning a format after one flop instead of checking execution.
- The millennial pause and dead air between phrases.
- Backlighting, dirty lens, earbud/phone-mic audio, voice quieter than music/effects.
- Leaving the top half of the frame empty; subtitles touching edges; title too big for the safe zone.
- Colorful boxed subtitles (dated) and heavy effects editing.
- Watermarked or low-resolution external clips.
- Switching to comedy just because the niche's top results are comedy.
- No CTA → views without followers.
- Buying bots; letting ads dominate account growth; paying ads to grow followers.
- Duplicating a profile (same photo, bio, email) when starting a new account; reposting old content on a growing account.
- Saving and skipping good videos on the research account.
- Over-obsessing over hashtags, trending audio, captions, background.
- Expecting fast results; trying to implement everything at once and freezing. Knowledge is only potential power; action with knowledge is power.
