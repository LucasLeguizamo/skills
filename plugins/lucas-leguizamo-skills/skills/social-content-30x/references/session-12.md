# Session 12 — Strategy, wrap-up and real-world challenges (Sesión 12 — Estrategia, cierre y retos reales)

Problem: people stall on theory, originality and "the right account" instead of publishing → the answer is volume: copy proven formats exactly, publish daily until you reach 100 videos, and only then improvise.

Closing session of the program, run as an open feedback clinic on real accounts across many verticals (restaurants, real estate, jewelry, event production, law, personal finance, leadership, automation).

## Key ideas
- Publishing is the whole game. "Si no está publicado, no existió" ("if it isn't published, it didn't exist"). One or two videos give you no data to decide anything.
- Copy the format faithfully, above all the first 3 seconds: on-screen text, text size, typeface, music and framing. Adding your own transitions or tweaks breaks what made the original go viral.
- Start with simple, low-editing formats taken from viral videos of accounts that are not huge (their success is more replicable than a mega-account's).
- Once a format works, keep it as the core and spend a minority share testing other niches/formats (80/20 split).
- Personal brand beats company account: company accounts grow harder than personal ones. Keep paid ads on the existing (business) account and open a separate organic account with the owner's face.
- Do not mix paid and organic on the same account: if both run together you cannot tell which one is failing.
- Position from real lived experience (e.g., leadership told from the analyst's seat, not from the top) and speak in plain language.
- Do not use AI avatars to build connection with an audience.
- Paid ads measure impressions and acquisition cost, not whether content connects. Do not use ads as a content-quality test.
- What you say matters more than the editing; first videos will be bad and that is fine.
- Answer comments: it builds engagement and comments are a source of content ideas.
- Descriptions/captions carry keywords for in-platform search (SEO); hashtags are not used.
- Set a daily publishing goal, not a weekly one, and commit publicly (e.g., announce "100 videos") to create accountability pressure.
- Frustration never goes away (instructors feel it too); consistency is what wins.
- If owners/managers refuse to be on camera: someone on the team starts a personal account, and once it works it pulls the others in.
- Outsource paid ads to an agency and replace it if it does not deliver. Marketing agencies that do not run their own social channels will fall behind.
- Counterpoint handled in class: participants argued for original content (own products, dishes, jokes, top-down leadership). The instructor's answer: with few videos published that is a belief without data; copy first, improvise on top of a format only after it is proven to work.

## Numbers & rules of thumb
- 100 videos = minimum sample to start understanding what works (copying formats and testing).
- First 3 seconds = the part of a format that must be copied most exactly.
- 80 % of output on the format that already works / 20 % testing other niches or formats.
- Trial reels only make sense when uploading more than 5 videos per day.
- Cadence: 1 video per day, starting today, until reaching 100.
- Hashtags: 0 (not used); keywords go in the description instead.

## Frameworks / step-by-step methods
The "100 videos" plan:
1. Publish one video today; then one every day until reaching 100. Make the goal daily and public.
2. Source formats from viral videos of small-to-mid accounts; prefer simple, low-edit formats.
3. Replicate the format exactly, especially the first 3 seconds (text, size, font, music, framing). No added transitions.
4. Write keyword-rich descriptions; skip hashtags.
5. Reply to comments and mine them for new video ideas.
6. When a format works, keep ~80 % on it and use ~20 % to test other niches/formats; only now improvise on top of it.

Account structure:
1. Leave paid ads on the existing business account (or hand ads to an agency).
2. Create a separate organic personal-brand account with the owner's (or a team member's) face.
3. Judge each account by its own metric: ads by impressions/CAC, organic by content traction.

Scaling production:
1. Hire an editor whose job is to clone formats faithfully.
2. Hire an assistant who scouts formats the creator likes.

If you have not published yet: identify what must change in your routine so that publishing (not perfection) is the daily objective.

## Pitfalls / what instructors warned against
- Theorizing or "creating original content" before having published enough to have data.
- Changing a copied format (custom transitions, different text/music/framing in the hook) — it kills what made it work.
- Starting with complex, heavy-editing formats.
- Mixing paid and organic on one account (you lose the ability to diagnose which is failing).
- Betting growth on a company account instead of a personal brand.
- Using AI avatars to connect with people.
- Reading ad results as a signal of content quality.
- Using trial reels at low volume (below 5 videos/day).
- Relying on hashtags.
- Waiting for perfect videos; weekly instead of daily goals.
- Keeping an agency that does not deliver; agencies that do not practice social media themselves.
