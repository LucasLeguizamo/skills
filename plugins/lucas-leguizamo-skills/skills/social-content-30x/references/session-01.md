# Session 1 — Mindset: why build on social media now (Sesión 1 — Mindset: por qué construir en redes hoy)

Problem: business owners stall on social because of limiting beliefs and a fear of "spamming" → Answer: treat media as one of only four scaling levers, rewrite those beliefs, and win with volume ("hasta en la sopa" — be everywhere).

Lead instructor: Andrés Bilbao (co-founder of Rappi and 30X). Format: a long talk on mindset (about 60–70 min), then open Q&A. The instructor said it was "not a frameworks class". The one thing to take away is believing you can build great social channels.

## Key ideas

- **Media is one of four ways to scale a business** (from Naval Ravikant): people (hiring), capital (banks, investors), code, and media. People and capital need someone else's permission and trust first. Code and media need no permission, so a nobody can scale with them. People is the lever that scales least.
- **The real work is changing limiting beliefs, not learning frameworks.** You can't get past the ceiling your beliefs set. The instructor claimed a whole business's ceiling can be explained by 2–4 deep beliefs, for example feeling like a thief for charging high margins, which keeps the business on thin margins.
- **The six common limiting beliefs and their rewrites:**
  1. "Content takes hours away from real work" → content *is* part of the work.
  2. "I'm not expert enough to have an opinion" → share what works *for you*. Personal experience is never a lie.
  3. "Friends and colleagues will mock me" → that's the cost of doing business. Everyone who creates gets mocked.
  4. "I'm not good on camera" → "not yet". Practice fixes it, and very few people start out good.
  5. "Posting often is spamming" → spam doesn't exist, especially on social. Ask how to post *more* with quality, not how to avoid spamming.
  6. "Offering my services online costs me dignity" → it's the cost of doing business, not undignified.
  - Deeper beliefs also matter: "I don't deserve this, it's too easy" (self-sabotage after success) and "the rich are bad" (a vow of poverty).
- **Volume beats optimization (the "Midwit Curve" applied to sales).** Geniuses and simpletons give the same answer: do more of what already works. Average people want to innovate. Exploration is only for finding a format that works. Once you find one, squeeze it dry.
- **"Volumen no quiere decir bajar calidad"** (volume doesn't mean lower quality). If 2 good Reels a day bring X growth, do 4 for double or 6 for triple. Ask "how do I make N pieces *with quality*?", not "will quality drop?"
- **Named strategies:** "Hasta en la sopa" means be everywhere, in maximum volume. "The Avengers" is a multi-face brand: a lead face plus several team members, each with their own growing account, all feeding the company brand.
- **Separate your identity from the algorithm.** The person who praises you isn't really talking to you, and neither is the one who insults you. Online you are a mirror: people react to their own beliefs. Think of yourself as a symbol (the instructor's analogy was Joan of Arc), not as a person.
- **Social needs high self-esteem.** Hostile comments and DMs at scale are guaranteed. If your self-esteem is fragile, work on it (therapy included). Insults only hurt where you already feel insecure.
- **Read rejection as proof of impact.** "La otra cara de la moneda del amor no es el odio. Es la indiferencia" (the opposite of love isn't hate, it's indifference). The enemy is anonymity, not haters.
- **The goal is to beat anonymity, not to "sell".** With a big enough funnel, people who fit the product buy it on their own. Example: a big audience raised the price of a board seat from about USD 3,000 (hard to get) to offers of about USD 12,000 (turned down).
- **Play an infinite game.** Social is measured in years and five-year periods. A long horizon is what lets you start on a platform where others already have millions of followers.
- **Build the habit "muscle".** Post, post, post. Remove friction: a permanent recording set at home, the camera you already have. "The best setup is the one you actually use." Don't be a perfectionist.
- **Pose vs. scale.** Avoid vanity metrics: a million followers is vanity. Real metrics are sales, then impact, happy customers, and repurchase. A super-niche channel with about 150k followers can outsell a 4M one.
- **Write from operating experience.** Share "this is what works for me" instead of lecturing. It connects better and protects you from being called a fraud.
- **Give away the insights, charge for the implementation.** Deep insights signal expertise. For consulting, give away about 95–100%. People will still pay you to do it.
- **Entertain, always.** People open Instagram or TikTok to be entertained, not to study.
- **DMs are where the business happens.** Social is the shop window or bridge, and deals close in DMs.
- **Platform roles:**
  - TikTok: a test lab. It doesn't care who you are, so every post starts from zero. Test lots of things there and move the winners to Instagram.
  - Instagram: the curated channel, and the one whose numbers count.
  - X and Facebook: just repost what you made for the other platforms. That alone can grow X a lot.
  - LinkedIn: write-first. It's a sniper rifle with a machine-gun magazine: a big audience makes cold DMs to anyone get answered. If you're B2B, skipping LinkedIn is irresponsible.
  - TikTok Shop: growing fast, worth exploring.
- **Organic first makes paid ads better.** Being good at organic lowers your paid CAC, because the creative skill is the same. TikTok trains it because you are always starting from zero.
- **Spread your focus only as far as your management capacity allows.** Running several brands or accounts is fine if you build content systems, not if you do everything yourself. If you have two different businesses, focus first on the one you most want to grow or the most profitable one.
- **"Follow orders first, innovate later."** "My case is different" is the most common objection, and it's almost always false. Your business is never as niche as you think.
- **Be a beginner on purpose.** Look at any creator's oldest videos: they were bad. They improved because they kept posting. During the program, treat it as fast exploration: push volume as high as you can, learn what works, repeat it. You aren't defining yourself yet.
- **Identity filter:** you won't keep making content you don't like. Pick the formats you like from the reference pool, mixed with your own identity.

## Numbers & rules of thumb

- 4 scaling levers: people, capital, code, media.
- 6 limiting beliefs to rewrite (listed above).
- A whole business can often be explained by 2–4 deep beliefs.
- Effort split: **80% more of the same, 10% improved, 10% new.**
- Sales math: 100 leads → about 33 conversations → 4 closes. An exceptional optimizer raising conversion by 20% gets 5 closes. Multiplying call volume by 10x gets about 48 closes.
- Example of a client's starting posture: 2 posts/day, pushed to 3–4/day. The instructor's line: 2 good Reels → do 4 to double growth, or 6 to triple it.
- Volume references: one instructor peaked at 40 videos/day. The team's aspirational target is 200/day. A media-company operator they studied posts about 250/day.
- Scaling path one instructor followed: 1 → 3 → 10 → 20 videos/day. Each step felt risky for quality, and it never actually dropped.
- If you have the capacity for 4 pieces a day, the 30X team would do 6.
- An instructor estimated that a small account posting 1/day consistently could pass 500k followers in a year.
- One reference account with about 70k followers had a Reel with about 3M views (it copied a proven format well).
- The best references are small accounts with outsized videos, for example about 15k followers with a 2M-view video.
- Hostile messages at scale: about 5,000 in 7 days during one backlash. Even 3 hostile comments can wreck the week of someone with fragile self-esteem.
- The instructor's team grew about 3M followers across platforms in about one year, starting from LinkedIn only.
- DM response time target: **about 1 minute** (automate with ManyChat).
- LinkedIn outreach: about 100 DMs/week, about 150/week if done very well (more across several accounts).
- Consulting content: give away about 95–100% of the know-how.
- Reference machine target: about 200 references/week.
- When starting out, the instructor initially rejected about 70% of the reference formats suggested to him. Later he learned to adapt them.
- About 2–3 months is the minimum before judging a LinkedIn machine.
- Small or new accounts: go 100% organic. You haven't "earned the right to burn money" on ads yet.
- Free-writing exercise: 5 minutes to surface deep beliefs (1 minute in class as a warm-up).
- Program structure: 6 weeks, 12 live sessions of about 2 h each.

## Frameworks / step-by-step methods

**Limiting-belief rewrite (the session's core homework, called "the most important thing")**
1. While the class runs, note every insecurity that comes up ("I feel insecure about…").
2. Free-write for about 5 minutes ("verbal diarrhea") until the deep beliefs come out. The deeper, the better. If the talk annoyed you, that's a sign it hit a real belief.
3. List each limiting belief about this specific project, one by one.
4. Rewrite each one as an empowering belief (use the six rewrites above as a model).
5. Keep noticing them. Once a belief is identified, you can work around it. Unidentified beliefs are "like swimming with a parachute".

**Volume decision**
1. Find what's working: a format, channel, or post type.
2. Put 80% of effort into doing more of it, 10% into improving it, and 10% into new things.
3. Pick an ambitious output number, then ask "how do we produce that *with quality*?"

**Copying references (the "copy the format, not the content" rule)**
1. A reference is a specific video that over-performed, not a creator you admire.
2. Take the idea and format, check that you agree, research it (add data or papers), write a similar script in your voice, and close with your own call to action.
3. Test on TikTok, then promote the best ones to Instagram.

**Paid vs. organic**
1. New or small account: organic only. Learn what connects.
2. If paid ads already drive the business, keep running them and use the organic lessons to improve the creative and cut cost.
3. Budget paid deliberately: it can make a team lazy if it becomes a crutch.
4. Attribution excuses don't hold up: ask every buyer how they found you (for example by a quick message).

**Funnel or offer questions (free 30-min call, pricing, and so on):** test it in the market, iterate, test again. Don't theorize.

## Pitfalls / what instructors warned against

- Treating content as separate from "real work".
- Asking "how do I avoid spamming?" instead of "how do I do more with quality?"
- Innovating constantly instead of exploiting what already works.
- Tying your self-worth to praise or insults. Posting with fragile self-esteem.
- **Never post when angry or resentful.** In the instructor's experience it always goes badly.
- Chasing vanity metrics (followers) over sales, impact, and repurchase.
- Being stingy with insights. Lecturing without real operating experience.
- Not entertaining: social isn't a classroom.
- Slow DM responses, which lose hot leads.
- Neglecting Stories because they feel like "posing". The instructor admitted this is his own weak spot. Stories build community.
- Ignoring LinkedIn as a B2B business.
- Spreading across too many brands or accounts beyond your management capacity, or doing all the content yourself instead of building a system.
- Relying on paid ads so much that you never learn whether your content connects, or buying followers, which does hurt the account.
- "My case is different" and "my niche is too small" thinking.
- Copying content 1:1 instead of adapting the format.
- Worrying now about year-two problems you don't have yet.
- Expecting quick results: most accounts don't take off fast, and the start is the hardest part.
