# Session 4 — State of the Art and Winning Strategies (Sesión 4 — Estado del arte y estrategias ganadoras)

Problem: beginners stall waiting for "more information" and try to invent content from scratch in a crowded feed → publish at volume, aim for visibility first, and copy proven formats filtered through objective / client / identity until you hit "content market fit".

## Key ideas

- **Volume is the strategy.** The goal is 100 videos; everything else is an excuse to avoid the hard part (sitting down and recording). "La información número uno es: no necesitas más información" (the #1 piece of information is that you don't need more information). Nothing taught in the course should become a prerequisite to start publishing.
- **Go intense, not slow.** Early frustration is unavoidable, so compress it: a few hard months instead of years of posting once a week.
- **Two possible objectives on social: visibility and conversion.** In the initial phase only visibility matters: get your client to *see* you, not yet to understand the product. Don't try to sell early; generate value.
- **But the profile must always be ready to sell** (your "landing page"): clear face photo, a name that gives context, a bio that says what you do / why to trust you / a CTA, a link, and pinned videos (who I am + one of the best). Publishing beats optimizing — never delay posting to polish the profile.
- **Give people a reason to follow in the bio**, beyond your product (e.g., a recurring free live session on a fixed weekday). The instructor claims this sharply raises the view-to-follow ratio.
- **Personal brand over company brand in ~99% of cases.** Ideal is both; if bandwidth allows only one, pick personal. On Instagram/TikTok a company account works if a real person (founder or team member) is the face; on LinkedIn essentially only personal brands work.
- **Three ways to generate value: entertain, teach, inspire.** Most strong accounts settle into one identity and blend the others in ("I teach while entertaining"), rather than rotating randomly.
- **The winning angle is rarely the obvious one.** Patterns from company examples:
  - A supermarket that never films in its stores; it runs an office-sitcom dynamic around its two managers.
  - A medical-office rental business that never teaches medicine; it entertains doctors (medical series, "celebrities you didn't know were doctors", which professions take longest to study).
  - A clothing brand whose designer-founder teaches the history of denim from inside the shop.
  - A video-production company showing behind-the-scenes tricks of how its ads are made.
  - A stock-investing app that runs a pure finance-education account and mentions the app in passing.
  - A large dairy brand mixing product trivia games with employees and TikTok trends.
  - A frozen-meals seller who can't show the product or the factory, so he films himself organizing his kitchen and freezing home food for his target buyer (women ~30–40 running a household).
  - A shipping company producing emotional mini-films "presented by" the brand without talking about shipping.
  - A seller whose viral personal account is one-line "friend's advice" clips; the business is discovered by curiosity.
  - None of these started that way; each is the evolution of hundreds or thousands of posts.
- **Optimize on what you already know.** A video that seems hard to you is trivial for someone inside that industry; build on your own expertise, not on what you think "should" work.
- **Production is not the differentiator.** Cheap mic, backlit, home setup, visible cables — hundreds of thousands of views anyway. What matters is the content and the authority the profile conveys.
- **Why copy formats now:** in 2021 merely being present was enough. Today a video depends on too many aligned variables (design, hook, duration, idea, storytelling, punchline/remate, how you film, eloquence, energy). Starting from zero is betting blind on all of them; copying a format that already worked means most are pre-solved. There are also far more viral formats to borrow from than before.
- **Same format, different results — because of profile authority, not setup.** Three creators copied one identical format; the one whose profile signalled the most credibility won by an order of magnitude despite the worst setup.
- **A video that goes viral is not automatically useful.** Always ask: does it meet my objective? Does it reach my client? A viral video that attracts the wrong audience brings "garbage" followers and low engagement on your real content.
- **Define the avatar in writing.** Usually the client who already buys from you (you know the most about them). If you can't write their profile, you don't know them yet — publish anyway; comments will tell you who is actually arriving.
- **Define identity:** what you will and won't do, your values, "what only I have". For companies, negotiate the brand's flexibility; legacy branding usually needs a refresh for social.
- **Copy the format, not the person.** Study many references in your niche; your identity becomes the mix of formats you chose. Prefer formats whose message you'd genuinely say yourself, then put it in your own words and examples.
- **Evolve copied formats into identity.** Case: a political campaign copied a viral clip (a woman chasing people who refused flyers). The 15-second remake was their first breakout; they iterated the gag (flyer → newspaper "slap" at things that are wrong in the city) until it became the campaign's signature way of elegantly calling out problems.
- **Content market fit** (analogy to product market fit): content that reaches your client, they love it, and you love it because it's your identity. Reach it only by publishing, comparing to objective and identity, keeping/refining or dropping each format.
- **A 100% clone of an account is possible and has worked** (e.g., a Spanish-language replica of a famous street-interview-with-millionaires channel; a local adaptation of a "buy all the vendor's stock and give it away" format). Not recommended, especially for companies, but valid. Adapt to local context.
- **Networks change; there is never a final copy-paste.** Keep testing and adapting, anchored on objective, client and identity.
- **Personal brands can't skip real-life work.** The only way to become unforgettable is to do unforgettable things offline and reflect them in content.
- **Speed before quality, with the decision-maker inside the team.** With company clients the instructor's only early metric is cycle time: idea today → filmed today → published today. The owner doesn't need to be on camera but must sit in the working group, so the team feels allowed to explore.
- **When to start selling:** once people love your content and the account grows, selling becomes an easier problem (add a CTA at the end, DMs, funnels — covered in a later session). Starting by selling skips needed learning.
- **Credentials belong up front.** If you have a strong track record, state it immediately (pinned "who I am" video, bio); refusing to is a limiting belief. If you have no track record, don't fake one — make the journey ("I'm starting/rebuilding, here's what I'm learning") the content. Youth or newness can be the differentiator vs. established incumbents.
- **Show, don't claim.** Don't say you're great; deliver value that proves it ("I did this, it worked, do it this way, here's a free resource").
- **Honest vulnerability connects; pure perfection feels fake; a brand that only suffers also fails.** Don't manufacture pity.
- **Platform rules are constraints, not fights.** If a content type keeps getting censored, drop it and find another format in your niche that you like.
- **Turn an accidental viral hook into a tool — or don't.** If something off-topic went viral (e.g., criticism of your accent, a personal diet), you can use it as the hook and pivot the body to your real topic, or start clean.
- **Stories (IG Stories) are very useful** as the place for links/CTAs and behind-the-scenes; treat each story as a value delivery, not a repost dump. Not a priority for beginners.
- **Social media won't fix a broken business.** "Instagram no le va a solucionar a uno el product market fit" (Instagram won't solve your product-market fit). It only gets you seen.
- **Prefer one big account over per-city/per-location fragments.** A city name in the handle limits reach; grow one large account and list locations in the bio.
- For event/concert promoters: the artist poster works only for headliners; also borrow formats from artists and music creators (artist history, genre content), carousels included, and collaborate with creators.

## Numbers & rules of thumb

- Goal: 100 videos. "Don't judge yourself at video 2 — judge yourself at video 100."
- ~80% of the path is creating/publishing the videos.
- Rhythm: ~7 videos per week (≈ 1/day); a week into the program you "should have ~10 videos".
- Intensity: ~3 hard months vs. suffering 1–2 years at 1 video/week.
- Personal brand over company brand in ~99% of cases.
- Bio = 3 parts (what you do & for whom / why trust you / CTA); pin ~2 videos (who I am + a top performer), swap them when better ones appear.
- Video length: ~40–45 s for beginners (start around 40 s); >45 s is hard when starting; follow the copied format's length (20 s, 60 s, 90 s are fine if that's the format).
- 2021 benchmark: a one-minute video put you above 99% of creators — no longer true.
- Same copied format by three creators: ~1,000 / ~3,400 / ~26,000 likes (≈20k / ≈100k / ≈2–2.4M views); the top one gained ~39,000 Instagram + ~14,000 TikTok followers.
- One example creator's style is the evolution of ~3,000+ (≈3,060) videos; another brand had ~517 videos on one platform.
- Political-campaign breakout: a 15-second copied clip → ~200,000 views when nothing else broke through.
- A large dairy brand example: ~400k Instagram, ~1M TikTok followers.
- Company velocity ramp: get to 2 videos/day, then ~10 tests per week (weekdays), review weekly.
- Wait ~50 videos before judging whether the account concept is right or needs a pivot.
- Under ~10,000 followers, restarting from zero "isn't serious"; 50–100 copied-format videos can get a new account there.
- If you make 100 copied-format videos, "it's impossible that none goes viral".
- A creator optimizing copied formats: ~70,000 followers in ~1 month.
- Full-clone example: original ~10M followers; the Spanish copy ~1.4M.
- Copy accusations become a real problem at large scale (~800,000 followers), not at the start.
- Off-topic viral hook example: ~2M views vs. ~40k typical; reused as hook → ~1M views.
- Emotional/pity-driven formats wear out in ~6 months; the product must carry it after.
- An AI-made video took ~3 days (sleepless) and was only worth it because the format was already proven; without that certainty the hit may come at iteration 20–30.
- Testing a doubt: try it 10 times in 10 days rather than debating it.

## Frameworks / step-by-step methods

**Account foundation**
1. Pick objective: visibility now, conversion later.
2. Decide personal vs. company brand (default personal; company only with a human face).
3. Choose the main value mode: entertain, teach, or inspire.
4. Write the avatar: (fake) name, age, job, pains, goals, interests.
5. Write identity: what you will / won't do, values, what only you have.
6. Optimize profile: face photo, context name, 3-part bio + reason to follow, link, 2 pinned videos.
7. Publish ~7/week; refine all of the above from comments and results.

**Format-copy loop (objective / client / identity triangle)**
1. Find formats that already worked in your niche (many references, not one person).
2. Copy the format, put it in your words, examples and experience.
3. Publish.
4. Check: did it meet my objective? Did it reach my client? Does it represent me (do I agree with it, am I comfortable)?
5. All three + performance → double down and evolve it toward a signature format. Viral but off-objective/off-identity → drop it. On-identity but not landing → also drop it.
6. Repeat until content market fit.

**Company content cadence**
1. Put the decision-maker in the content team.
2. Optimize for cycle time (idea → film → publish same day).
3. Ramp to 2 videos/day, then run ~10 tests/week and review weekly.

**Pinned "who I am" video**
- Personal brand: an executive summary of your credentials and what you teach/sell. Company: what you are, what you do, plus a trust signal (press, rankings, client count). A carousel also works. Replace it when a better one exists.

## Pitfalls / what instructors warned against

- Waiting for more information, tools or a perfect profile before publishing.
- Posting slowly (1/week) and stretching the frustration over years.
- Trying to sell before the audience loves the content.
- Inventing content from scratch when proven formats exist.
- Over-investing in production (lighting, setup); it isn't what makes videos perform.
- Chasing virality that doesn't reach your client or meet your objective.
- Copying a single person, or copying a message you don't agree with — when it goes viral the hate lands because "you feel it deep down".
- Hiding credentials out of fear of seeming arrogant; or, conversely, inventing credentials you don't have.
- Manufactured pity, or a brand that only ever suffers.
- Fighting platform censorship instead of switching formats.
- Splitting into one account per city/location when one large account would grow faster.
- Expensive AI-video productions without a proven format and skills.
- Expecting social media to solve product-market fit or a business problem.
- Avoiding the camera indefinitely: faceless content is acceptable as a first step, but not appearing yourself wastes a big opportunity; limiting beliefs and past bad experiences are cured only by volume.
